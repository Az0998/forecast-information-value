Operational nested river gauges are not the same object as lumped rainfall–runoff catchments. Tomorrow’s discharge at a forecast point is already partly written in today’s upstream hydrograph. We estimate when that nested information still has value on 21 documented United States Geological Survey and Water Survey of Canada triads (training through 2018, validation 2019–2020, testing 2021–2023). Instruments are lagged-upstream linear routing, gradient-boosted trees, persistence, an ordinary-least-squares precipitation ceiling, and the critical success index on floods above the training 90th percentile, with block-bootstrap intervals. Extra-gauge skill falls as one-day routing Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) approaches one (grouped-climate regime coefficient -0.26, coefficient of determination R² = 0.63). The James still gains from upstream gauges (change in NSE of +0.111) while the Willamette gains only +0.007; on the Rio Grande the extra-gauge term is negative. Event scores can disagree with NSE. Three-day unused rain is large on some humid main stems and indistinguishable from zero on snowmelt. Nested gauges therefore have a regime of information value; they should not be scored as if they were lumped rainfall–runoff catchments.

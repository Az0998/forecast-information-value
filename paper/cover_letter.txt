Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript

“Information bottlenecks, not architectures: a cross-media protocol for mid-range environmental forecasts”

for publication in Ecological Informatics as a Research paper.

The manuscript is not another LSTM-on-a-basin or Transformer-on-a-shelf case study. It proposes a four-part evaluation grammar (domain-simple baseline, event score, information ceiling, failure locus) and applies that grammar to five climate-contrasting USGS rivers, a nested Water Survey of Canada Bow River transfer, and a WOA-informed East China Sea shelf oxygen cube. Learned models add decision skill only while the limiting input is present.

Headline tests: (i) upstream-gauge value is climate-conditional (James ΔNSE +0.13 versus Willamette ≈0); (ii) on the semi-arid Verde, XGBoost beats linear routing at 1 day while LSTM-Attention loses, then XGBoost collapses at 3–7 days; (iii) operational GFS precipitation is negative at 3 days on the Potomac while oracle rain still lifts the ceiling; (iv) an ordinary-least-squares precipitation ceiling is ~0 on snowmelt (Animas, Bow) and positive on the Potomac and Verde, so the snowmelt result is not an XGBoost artefact; (v) a spatiotemporal Transformer beats climatology at 1-month oxygen lead and should yield to a hybrid/climatology thereafter, with residuals concentrated on the coastal shelf. Low-oxygen scores use the training 10th percentile on a development cube, not hypoxia <60 µmol kg⁻¹.

We confirm this is not a dual submission with the companion Mask-View missingness manuscript. Code and frozen tables: https://github.com/Az0998/forecast-information-value
